The maze is secretly computer science

A data structure is a way to keep things organized, like a pile of plates or a line of people. An algorithm is a recipe of steps to solve a problem. This game used both:

In the game Computer science name Think of it as

How the ghost finds you Queue + BFS A line at the canteen

Your run

The picture above shows where you walked. Brighter red means you walked there more than once.

Why twice? You hit a dead end and turned around. That is called backtracking, and you do it in real life too, like after a wrong turn on the way to school.

Best possible steps:

The map (Graph)

Imagine a school map. Every room is a dot and every hallway is a line. A map made of dots and lines is called a graph.

Your maze is a graph. Every open tile is a dot, and every step between two tiles is a line. A wall just means "no line here".

Press Replay above to watch the dots light up. Subway maps, GPS roads and friends on social media are graphs too!

Building the maze (Stack + DFS)

The maze was dug like exploring a cave: keep walking forward, and when you hit a dead end, go back to the last spot that had another tunnel. This "go deep first" recipe is called DFS.

To remember where to go back to, the computer uses a stack, like a pile of plates. Walk forward = put a plate on top. Stuck = take the top plate off. The last plate on is the first plate off.

Press Replay to see it. Bright red tiles are the plates in the pile.

You use a stack every day: the Undo button and the Back button in your browser.

The ghost (Queue + BFS)

Drop a pebble in a pond and the ripple spreads out: everything 1 step away, then 2 steps away, then 3. The ghost finds you the same way. This is called BFS, and it always finds the shortest way to you.

To remember what to check next, the computer uses a queue, like the line at the canteen. The first one in line is the first one served.

Press Replay to see it. White tiles are the people waiting in line.

You see this in real life: "People you may know" on social media checks your friends first, then their friends.
